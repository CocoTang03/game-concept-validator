# Portfolio Rubric

## Purpose

This rubric is fixed across the evaluation.
Definitions and default weights remain stable.
Scores may change because the concept or evidence changes.

# Scoring Dimensions

Score each from 1–10.

## A. Player Hook — 14%
How quickly can someone understand why this game may be interesting?

## B. Differentiation — 10%
How distinct is the concept from nearby games?

## C. Core Experience Strength — 14%
How compelling is the Player Promise if successfully executed?

## D. Prototype Speed — 11%
How quickly can the Riskiest Assumption be meaningfully tested?

## E. Scope Control — 11%
How naturally bounded is the project?

## F. Production Feasibility — 9%
How realistic is production given known team/resources?

## G. Content Burden — 7%
Score HIGH when content burden is LOW.

## H. Market Readability — 7%
Can likely players understand the game quickly?

## I. Team / Personal Fit — 5%
How well does it fit current skills, collaborators, tools, motivation, and preferred development style?

## J. Validation Cost — 4%
Score HIGH when major uncertainty can be tested cheaply.

## K. Competitive Whitespace — 8%
How much meaningful design space remains relative to Prior Art?

# Default Formula

```text
Portfolio Score =
Player Hook              × 0.14
+ Differentiation        × 0.10
+ Core Experience        × 0.14
+ Prototype Speed        × 0.11
+ Scope Control          × 0.11
+ Production Feasibility × 0.09
+ Content Burden         × 0.07
+ Market Readability     × 0.07
+ Team / Personal Fit    × 0.05
+ Validation Cost        × 0.04
+ Competitive Whitespace × 0.08
```

# Hard Gates

## Scope Gate
RED when the smallest satisfying version still requires multiple gameplay modes, large narrative production, large art burden, heavy infrastructure, or extensive content before core value appears.

## Identity Gate
RED when removing theme/art leaves little differentiation, the hook is mainly a feature collection, or there is no clear Design Nucleus.

## Prior Art Gate
RED when multiple existing games already match the Design Nucleus, differentiation is mostly surface-level, or the closest alternative already satisfies the same Player Promise more clearly.

## Evidence Gate
YELLOW when the idea sounds strong but the dominant assumption is still Unverified.

Do not hide a RED Gate behind a high weighted average.
