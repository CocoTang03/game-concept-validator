# Schell Design Review

## Purpose
Perform a qualitative deep design review after portfolio filtering and adversarial refinement.
Do not convert this into another weighted portfolio score.

# 1. Experience Lens
Ask what the player should feel moment to moment and what they should remember after the session.

# 2. Player Fantasy
Ask who the player feels they are, or what capability they feel they possess.

# 3. Four Elements
Review:

- Mechanics
- Story
- Aesthetics
- Technology

Then ask whether all four serve the same Player Promise.

# 4. Core Loop Stress Test
Test the loop at first 30 seconds, 5 minutes, 20 minutes, and late game.
Look for novelty decay and lack of mastery.

# 5. Player Motivation
Identify the primary motivation: Curiosity, Mastery, Discovery, Narrative, Collection, Expression, Sociality, Optimization, or Challenge.
Ask why the player continues after novelty fades.

# 6. Meaningful Choice
Check what decisions matter, what information informs them, and whether consequences are understandable.

# 7. Feedback
Check whether action produces readable feedback and whether feedback reinforces the Player Fantasy.

# 8. Interest Curve
Map:

```text
Hook
↓
Learning
↓
Variation
↓
Escalation
↓
Payoff
```

# 9. Skill vs Chance
Determine what the player learns, what improves through practice, what remains uncertain, and whether outcomes feel earned.

# 10. Theme–Mechanic Coherence
Ask whether mechanics actually express the claimed theme.

# 11. Accessibility
Check dependencies on hearing, color, reaction speed, fine motor control, reading speed, or specific hardware.

# 12. Final Schell Summary
Return:

**Intended Experience:**  
**Strongest Element:**  
**Weakest Element:**  
**Four Elements Coherence:** Low / Medium / High  
**Motivation Strength:** Low / Medium / High  
**Core Loop Sustainability:** Low / Medium / High  
**Interest Curve Risk:** Low / Medium / High  
**Main Contradiction:**  
**Overall Design Confidence:** Low / Medium / High
